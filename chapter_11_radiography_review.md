# Chapter 11 — plain radiography review

All 89 entries refer to recognized entities. Each row below reproduces the chapter pairing with an editorial assessment. The sources listed at the end support selected qualifications. This review does not establish individual guideline approval for every use.

## Chest X-rays (19)

| Entry | Finding in the chapter | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Pneumonia | Consolidation, air bronchograms | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Pulmonary tuberculosis | Cavitation, upper lobe opacities | Upper-lobe cavitation is a classic pattern. Confirmation of active TB requires additional evaluation. |
| Pneumothorax | Visible pleural line | Specify a visceral pleural line with absent peripheral vascular markings. |
| Pleural effusion | Meniscus sign | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Pulmonary edema | Bat-wing opacities | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Hemothorax | Pleural fluid opacity | The film shows pleural fluid. The opacity alone cannot establish that the fluid is blood. |
| Atelectasis | Pulmonary volume loss | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Emphysema | Hyperinflation, flattened diaphragm | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Lung abscess | Cavity with air-fluid level | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Lung cancer | Pulmonary mass or nodule | A nodule or mass raises suspicion and requires characterization. |
| Cardiomegaly | Enlarged cardiac silhouette | Classify as a finding. Portable anteroposterior views can magnify the cardiac silhouette. |
| Congestive heart failure | Pulmonary vascular congestion | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Sarcoidosis | Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Silicosis | Multiple small pulmonary nodules | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Asbestosis | Interstitial opacities, pleural plaques | Pleural plaques indicate asbestos exposure. Describe basal interstitial fibrosis separately. |
| Pulmonary fibrosis | Reticular opacities | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| ARDS | Bilateral pulmonary opacities | Expand acute respiratory distress syndrome. Bilateral opacities are one component of the clinical diagnosis. |
| Diaphragmatic hernia | Abdominal contents within the thorax | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Foreign body aspiration | Visible foreign body or air trapping | Many objects are radiolucent. Air trapping can be indirect evidence, and films can be normal. |

## Bone X-rays (26)

| Entry | Finding in the chapter | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Transverse fracture | Horizontal fracture line | Define the line as perpendicular to the long axis of the bone. Page orientation is incidental. |
| Oblique fracture | Diagonal fracture line | Describe the line relative to the long axis of the bone. |
| Spiral fracture | Spiral fracture line | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Comminuted fracture | Multiple bone fragments | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Greenstick fracture | Incomplete cortical fracture | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Torus fracture | Cortical buckling | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Avulsion fracture | Detached bone fragment | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Impacted fracture | Overlapping bone fragments | One fragment is driven into another. Simple overlap on a projection is insufficient. |
| Stress fracture | Periosteal reaction or fracture line | The listed changes can be absent early. |
| Osteomyelitis | Bone destruction, periosteal reaction | Periosteal reaction and bone destruction may appear after symptom onset. Early films can be normal. |
| Osteomalacia | Looser zones | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Rickets | Metaphyseal cupping and fraying | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Paget disease | Cortical thickening, bone expansion | Specify Paget disease of bone. |
| Osteosarcoma | Sunburst periosteal reaction | Sunburst reaction is an aggressive pattern shared by other lesions. |
| Ewing sarcoma | Onion-skin periosteal reaction | Layered periosteal reaction is a clue with other possible causes. |
| Chondrosarcoma | Rings-and-arcs calcifications | Rings and arcs indicate cartilage matrix and occur in benign tumors as well. |
| Osteochondroma | Bony projection | Look for continuity of cortex and medullary cavity with the parent bone. |
| Giant cell tumor | Expansile lytic lesion | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Unicameral bone cyst | Radiolucent bone lesion | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Aneurysmal bone cyst | Expansile radiolucent lesion | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Fibrous dysplasia | Ground-glass appearance | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Osteopetrosis | Increased bone density | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Osteogenesis imperfecta | Multiple fractures, osteopenia | Multiple fractures and osteopenia have a differential diagnosis. |
| Avascular necrosis | Subchondral collapse | Collapse is advanced. MRI can detect disease when films are normal. |
| Bone metastases | Lytic or sclerotic lesions | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Achondroplasia | Shortened long bones | Specify disproportionate proximal limb shortening. |

## Joint X-rays (15)

| Entry | Finding in the chapter | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Osteoarthritis | Joint space narrowing, osteophytes | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | Marginal erosions | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Psoriatic arthritis | Pencil-in-cup deformity | Pencil-in-cup is an advanced deformity. |
| Gout | Erosions with overhanging edges | Overhanging erosions generally reflect established disease. |
| Pseudogout | Chondrocalcinosis | Use calcium pyrophosphate deposition (CPPD) for the deposition process. Chondrocalcinosis can occur without an acute arthritis attack. |
| Septic arthritis | Joint space loss, bone destruction | Destructive changes can be late. A normal radiograph does not exclude infection. Joint fluid analysis is central to diagnosis. |
| Ankylosing spondylitis | Sacroiliac joint fusion | Sacroiliac fusion is an advanced structural change. |
| Charcot arthropathy | Joint fragmentation | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Joint dislocation | Loss of joint alignment | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Joint subluxation | Partial joint displacement | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Calcific tendinitis | Tendon calcification | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Developmental hip dysplasia | Abnormal acetabular development | Ultrasound is preferred in young infants. Pelvic radiography becomes useful as the hip ossifies. |
| Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease | Femoral head fragmentation | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Slipped capital femoral epiphysis | Epiphyseal displacement | Recognized plain-film association. Keep as an example. |
| Blount disease | Proximal tibial deformity | Describe medial proximal tibial deformity with varus. |

## Kidney, ureter and bladder X-rays (KUB) (5)

| Entry | Finding in the chapter | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Kidney stones | Radiopaque calculi | Only sufficiently radiopaque stones are visible. Uric acid stones are usually radiolucent. |
| Ureteral stones | Calcifications along ureter | Phleboliths can mimic distal ureteral calculi. |
| Bladder stones | Pelvic radiopaque calculi | Pelvic calcification alone does not localize a calculus to the bladder. |
| Staghorn calculus | Branching renal calculus | A morphological subtype of renal calculus, overlapping the kidney stone row. |
| Nephrocalcinosis | Renal parenchymal calcifications | Parenchymal calcium deposition has several possible underlying causes. |
