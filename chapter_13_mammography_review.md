# Chapter 13 review — Mammography

All 80 catalogue entries describe recognized diseases, pathological changes, imaging observations, or anatomical features. I found no fabricated condition. The nine section totals agree with the source tables. Several entries describe the same observable feature, so 80 is a count of rows and cannot serve as a count of diagnoses distinguishable on a mammogram.

## Highest-priority revisions

1. **State what a negative mammogram can miss.** In inflammatory breast cancer, skin and trabecular thickening can resemble mastitis, and a discrete mass may be absent. For persistent unilateral changes of the nipple suggestive of Paget disease, even a normal mammogram leaves the nipple lesion unresolved. Examination and biopsy address questions the images cannot answer. [NCI on inflammatory breast cancer](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/breast-cancer-types/inflammatory) · [NCI on Paget disease](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/breast-cancer-types/paget-disease-breast)
2. **Update the BI-RADS vocabulary and risk table.** The American College of Radiology (ACR) retired “developing asymmetry” as a standalone descriptor. Describe the asymmetry and document its interval change. Use “layering” for the mammographic appearance of milk of calcium and “coarse” for classic dystrophic or popcorn-like deposits. Category 3 has a lower bound above zero and is ordinarily assigned after diagnostic evaluation. [ACR changes in v2025](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/americancoldf5f-acrorgf92a-productioncb02-3650/media/ACR/Files/RADS/BI-RADS/BIRADS-v2025-Whats-New.pdf) · [ACR mammography lexicon and assessments](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/americancoldf5f-acrorgf92a-productioncb02-3650/media/ACR/Files/RADS/BI-RADS/BI-RADS-Summary-Form-Mammography.pdf)
3. **Name the reference test in ambiguous rows.** A circumscribed mass can be a simple cyst, a cyst containing debris, or a solid lesion. Mammography depicts its contour. Ultrasound characterizes its contents. Likewise, calcifications may identify the biopsy target that yields atypical ductal hyperplasia, flat epithelial atypia, or DCIS. They do not establish which lesion is present. [ACR/RSNA breast ultrasound](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/breastus) · [American Society of Breast Surgeons guide](https://www.breastsurgeons.org/docs/statements/asbrs-high-risk-lesions.pdf)
4. **Distinguish tumor appearance from grade and behavior.** Both benign and malignant phyllodes tumors can be large and lobulated. Grade requires pathological assessment. Qualify the benign-table entry as *benign adenomyoepithelioma* because atypical and malignant forms also occur. [Comparative phyllodes tumor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8539362/) · [WHO classification authors on adenomyoepithelioma](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13341065/)
5. **Qualify post-treatment and implant findings.** Skin and trabecular thickening after radiotherapy can reflect edema. Describe later fibrosis with retraction or volume loss and compare the treated breast over time. Visible silicone beyond the fibrous capsule supports extracapsular silicone, while mammography can miss a rupture confined within that capsule. [Serial mammographic study after breast conservation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8659366/) · [ACR breast implant evaluation](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/3100728/Narrative)

## Row-by-row assessment

The notes cover the existing catalogue in source order. The references address the specified clinical or terminology questions. They do not independently validate a diagnostic indication for every row.

### Malignant breast diseases (14 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Invasive ductal carcinoma | An irregular mass with spiculated margins is a valid example. Where intended, identify invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (often called invasive ductal carcinoma). Several other diagnoses can produce spiculation. |
| Invasive lobular carcinoma | Distortion or subtle asymmetry is appropriate. Some invasive lobular cancers are mammographically occult. |
| Ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) | Fine pleomorphic or fine linear/branching calcifications can raise suspicion for DCIS, especially in a ductal distribution. Some DCIS is noncalcified. Biopsy establishes in situ disease. |
| Tubular carcinoma | A small spiculated mass is plausible. Its resemblance to other invasive tumors or a radial scar limits specificity. |
| Mucinous carcinoma | A circumscribed or lobulated mass is plausible, particularly in pure mucinous carcinoma. A smooth margin alone does not establish benignity. |
| Medullary-pattern carcinoma | A real histological pattern. A rounded noncalcified mass can occur. State that medullary features are determined on tissue. |
| Papillary carcinoma | This umbrella covers multiple papillary entities. A discrete mass is plausible, and tissue assessment distinguishes them. |
| Metaplastic breast carcinoma | A dense irregular mass is possible. Circumscribed masses also occur. The image cannot identify metaplastic differentiation. |
| Inflammatory breast cancer | Skin and trabecular thickening reflect edema shared with infection. Rapid clinical onset and biopsy evidence of invasive disease are central. A mammogram may show no discrete mass. |
| Paget disease of the breast | Imaging can reveal an underlying mass or suspicious calcifications. The symptomatic nipple can have a normal mammographic appearance. Nipple or areolar skin biopsy establishes Paget disease. |
| Malignant phyllodes tumor | Large size and lobulation are compatible with phyllodes tumor. Histological criteria distinguish benign, borderline, and malignant tumors. |
| Primary breast lymphoma | A dense noncalcified mass is plausible and nonspecific. Tissue diagnosis and staging establish lymphoma and its primary breast origin. |
| Breast angiosarcoma | A mass or asymmetry is plausible. The finding can be subtle and cannot establish a vascular malignancy. |
| Metastases to the breast | Single or multiple rounded masses can occur. Clinical history and pathology distinguish metastasis from a new breast primary. |

### Precancerous and high-risk breast lesions (7 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Atypical ductal hyperplasia (ADH) | Grouped calcifications may be sampled and yield ADH. Mammographic appearance overlaps with DCIS. |
| Atypical lobular hyperplasia (ALH) | ALH is often incidental on biopsy. Correlate the sampled lesion with the imaging target before attributing calcifications to ALH. |
| Lobular carcinoma in situ (LCIS) | Distinguish classic LCIS, often incidental or occult, from pleomorphic or florid LCIS, which may present with suspicious calcifications. |
| Flat epithelial atypia | Amorphous calcifications are a possible biopsy target. Flat epithelial atypia is a pathological diagnosis. |
| Radial scar | Radiating lines and distortion are valid findings that can mimic carcinoma. This is a benign sclerosing lesion. Atypia and sampling concordance influence risk assessment. |
| Complex sclerosing lesion | This overlaps with radial scar. The term traditionally denotes a larger lesion, often greater than 1 cm. Avoid counting the two as readily separable image classes. |
| Atypical papilloma | Prefer papilloma with atypia. The mass or calcifications can prompt sampling. The atypia is identified on tissue. |

[American Society of Breast Surgeons guide to benign and high-risk lesions](https://www.breastsurgeons.org/docs/statements/asbrs-high-risk-lesions.pdf)

### Benign breast tumors and masses (12 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Fibroadenoma | An oval circumscribed mass is typical but shared with cysts and other tumors. |
| Involuting fibroadenoma | Coarse calcification in a degenerating mass is characteristic. Popcorn-like remains a useful visual description alongside the current lexicon term coarse. |
| Breast hamartoma | A circumscribed mass containing fat and fibroglandular tissue, sometimes with a thin pseudocapsule, is a useful mammographic example. |
| Breast lipoma | A circumscribed fat-density mass is characteristic. |
| Benign phyllodes tumor | Circumscription and lobulation can occur across phyllodes grades and overlap with fibroadenoma. Large size is optional. |
| Intraductal papilloma | A central papilloma may present as a retroareolar mass or duct abnormality. Small papillomas can be occult. |
| Granular cell tumor | A genuine, usually benign tumor that can appear spiculated and mimic invasive carcinoma. Diagnosis requires tissue. |
| Pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia (PASH) | Mass-forming PASH can appear circumscribed or as an asymmetry. Microscopic PASH may be incidental. |
| Breast myofibroblastoma | Prefer circumscribed soft-tissue-density mass. Mammographic density alone does not establish solid internal contents or myofibroblastoma histology. |
| Breast hemangioma | A circumscribed mass with occasional calcification is possible. Neither feature establishes its vascular origin. |
| Adenomyoepithelioma | Qualify as benign adenomyoepithelioma in this section. A mass with this appearance does not determine the lesion’s biological behavior. |
| Tubular adenoma | An often circumscribed mass is a reasonable description. Calcifications can occur, and tissue identifies tubular adenoma. |

[WHO classification update](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13341065/) · [Phyllodes cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8539362/)

### Cystic and fibrocystic breast conditions (8 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Simple breast cyst | Mammography can show a circumscribed mass. Targeted ultrasound can demonstrate the fluid contents and features of a simple cyst. |
| Complicated breast cyst | Internal debris distinguishes a complicated cyst from a simple cyst on ultrasound. This cannot be established from the mammographic outline. A complex cystic and solid mass is a different finding. |
| Fibrocystic breast changes | This broad pathological category includes several overlapping findings. Specify the actual observed change when annotating an image. |
| Galactocele | A fat-fluid level is characteristic when present, particularly in a lactating or recently lactating patient. Many galactoceles lack a visible level. |
| Oil cyst | A fat-density mass with a thin calcified rim is characteristic and overlaps with the fat-necrosis entry. |
| Apocrine metaplasia | Usually a histological observation, often in a cystic process. Masses or calcifications alone do not identify apocrine metaplasia. |
| Sclerosing adenosis | Distortion, calcifications, or a mass may mimic carcinoma and lead to biopsy. |
| Breast adenosis | Adenosis is a broader pathology term that includes sclerosing adenosis. Focal asymmetry and calcifications are nonspecific. |

[ACR/RSNA breast ultrasound](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/breastus)

### Inflammatory and infectious conditions (8 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Mastitis | Skin thickening and increased density support an inflammatory process. Persistent or progressive findings warrant reassessment for an underlying malignancy. |
| Breast abscess | A focal density with edema can suggest an abscess. Targeted ultrasound can demonstrate the collection. |
| Granulomatous mastitis | Asymmetry or irregular masses are plausible and can resemble invasive disease. Tissue sampling helps establish the diagnosis and investigate other causes. |
| Periductal mastitis | A retroareolar asymmetry or duct change is plausible. Site and symptoms add context, and mammography alone cannot identify the cause of inflammation. |
| Mammary duct ectasia | Dilated ducts and smooth large rod-like calcifications are recognized findings. A calcified duct does not by itself demonstrate active infection. |
| Breast tuberculosis | A real, uncommon infection. Masses and skin thickening can mimic cancer. Tissue and microbiological evaluation distinguish causes. |
| Diabetic mastopathy | An asymmetry, dense tissue, or an occult lesion can occur. History of diabetes and suspicious imaging alone cannot establish diabetic mastopathy. |
| Breast edema | A pattern of skin and trabecular thickening with several causes, including treatment effects, infection, and tumor-related lymphatic obstruction. Classify as a finding. |

[Clinical series of breast tuberculosis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11916200/) · [NCI on inflammatory breast cancer](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/breast-cancer-types/inflammatory)

### Traumatic and postoperative conditions (7 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Fat necrosis | Oil cysts and rim calcification can be characteristic. An irregular or spiculated appearance may instead require tissue evaluation. |
| Breast hematoma | A mass or asymmetry is plausible. Timing, trauma or surgery, and evolution on prior examinations support the interpretation. |
| Postoperative seroma | Describe a circumscribed mass at the operative site. Ultrasound demonstrates the fluid character when uncertain. |
| Postoperative scarring | Distortion at the surgical bed is expected and should be compared with prior studies. New or increasing distortion deserves a separate assessment. |
| Radiation-induced fibrosis | Skin and trabecular thickening are common manifestations of post-treatment edema. Later volume loss, retraction, and distortion can support fibrosis. Interval change matters. |
| Silicone granuloma | Dense extracapsular silicone may form nodules. The image shows silicone deposition and cannot establish a histological granulomatous reaction by itself. |
| Extracapsular breast implant rupture | Specify a silicone implant and silicone beyond the fibrous capsule. Consider prior rupture or explantation when attributing deposits to the current implant. A negative mammogram cannot establish implant integrity. |

### Breast calcification patterns (11 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Vascular calcifications | Parallel calcified vessel walls are characteristic. Keep as a mammographic finding. |
| Dermal calcifications | Superficial location and lucent centers support skin calcifications. A tangential view can establish skin location when uncertain. |
| Dystrophic calcifications | Use coarse as the current benign morphology. Dystrophic describes a process, often following tissue injury. Distinguish this from suspicious coarse heterogeneous deposits. |
| Secretory calcifications | Use large rod-like as the morphologic descriptor. Secretory or duct ectasia explains a common association. |
| Milk of calcium | Use layering as the descriptor and retain milk of calcium as the familiar name. Dependent layering on a true lateral view helps identify sediment. |
| Rim calcifications | A thin calcified shell around a lesion, often an oil cyst, is a valid typically benign pattern. |
| Amorphous calcifications | A recognized suspicious morphology. Hazy particles lack a clearly definable shape. Include their distribution. |
| Coarse heterogeneous calcifications | A recognized suspicious morphology. Irregular particles differ from larger, confluent, typically benign coarse calcifications. |
| Fine pleomorphic calcifications | Variation in size and shape is characteristic. Morphology and distribution guide suspicion. Histology establishes a diagnosis. |
| Fine linear calcifications | Use the complete descriptor fine linear or fine linear-branching. Describe linear or segmental distribution separately from particle shape. |
| Suture calcifications | Calcification along a suture, sometimes showing a knot, is a recognized postoperative finding. |

### Structural and anatomical abnormalities (10 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Architectural distortion | Converging lines or tissue retraction without a definite mass are appropriate. Tomosynthesis can reveal subtle distortion. Scar and malignancy are among the causes. |
| Focal asymmetry | Describe a localized asymmetry seen on at least two projections and occupying less than a quadrant. A one-view density has a different descriptor. |
| Global asymmetry | Describe asymmetrically distributed tissue involving at least one quadrant. Interval change and associated suspicious findings affect interpretation. |
| Developing asymmetry | The observation of a new or more conspicuous asymmetry remains important. In BI-RADS v2025, name the asymmetry and record interval change separately. |
| Nipple retraction | Specify whether retraction is new and unilateral. Longstanding inversion has different significance. |
| Skin thickening | A real associated finding. Distribution, interval change, and any underlying lesion help distinguish causes. |
| Axillary lymphadenopathy | Describe visible nodal enlargement or altered shape or fatty hilum. Ultrasound can better characterize the cortex. Enlargement alone cannot establish metastasis. |
| Intramammary lymphadenopathy | Compare size and morphology with prior images. A small oval intramammary node with a fatty hilum is a common benign finding. |
| Dense breast tissue | A breast-composition category. BI-RADS C and D are dense. Dense tissue can obscure cancer and is associated with increased risk. |
| Accessory breast tissue | A recognized anatomical variant, often axillary. Distinguish separate accessory tissue from the normal contiguous axillary tail. |

## Reporting and dataset sections

**BI-RADS assessment.** Give the reason for category 0, either further imaging or comparison with prior mammograms. Categories 1 and 2 both carry essentially zero likelihood in the assessment table, while a negative examination can still miss cancer. Enter the category 3 range as greater than 0% through 2%. Show explicit upper bounds for 4A (10%) and 4B (50%), and keep 4C below 95%. The chapter correctly places exactly 95% in category 5. Category 6 records a known, biopsy-proven malignancy, so a predictive probability is inapplicable. [ACR assessment table, page 4](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/americancoldf5f-acrorgf92a-productioncb02-3650/media/ACR/Files/RADS/BI-RADS/BI-RADS-Summary-Form-Mammography.pdf)

**Datasets.** All three named mammography datasets are genuine. CBIS-DDSM is derived from scanned film, which matters in a chapter focused on digital acquisition and tomosynthesis. VinDr-Mammo provides radiologists’ assessments and finding annotations. INbreast contains full-field digital mammograms. None of those descriptions establishes tomosynthesis coverage. TCIA is a collection host, PubMed Central is a literature archive, Radiopaedia is a case resource, and ACR provides reporting standards. [CBIS-DDSM collection record](https://wiki.cancerimagingarchive.net/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=52764371) · [VinDr-Mammo dataset record](https://www.physionet.org/content/vindr-mammo/1.0.0/) · [INbreast original publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22078258/)

**Annotation.** Retain the chapter’s distinction between image finding, radiologist assessment, and confirmed diagnosis. For example, label a group of calcifications as the observed finding, record BI-RADS as the assessment, and attach ADH or DCIS only when pathology from the corresponding target supports it. Record the actual reference standard when benign findings are established by ultrasound or follow-up. Preserve the original BI-RADS version for older datasets and document any mapping to v2025. [Breast Surgeons guidance on imaging–pathology concordance](https://www.breastsurgeons.org/docs/statements/asbrs-high-risk-lesions.pdf) · [VinDr-Mammo annotation methods](https://www.physionet.org/content/vindr-mammo/1.0.0/)
