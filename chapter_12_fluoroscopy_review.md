# Chapter 12 review — Fluoroscopy

I reviewed the condition and the stated fluoroscopic finding in all 103 rows across 11 anatomical sections. The section counts match the summary. I found no fabricated condition. The total counts catalogue entries, including observations such as laryngeal penetration and overlapping diagnoses such as carpal and scapholunate instability.

## Corrections to prioritize

1. **Uterine anomalies.** Hysterosalpingography (HSG) outlines the cavity. Both septate and bicornuate uteri can produce a divided cavity. Classification needs an assessment of the outer fundal contour, typically with three-dimensional transvaginal ultrasound. [ASRM guideline](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-diagnosis-and-treatment-for-uterine-septum-a-guideline-2024/)
2. **Suspected perforation and rupture.** Name the examination alongside the sign. Esophageal contrast leakage calls for a dedicated leak study. Traumatic bladder rupture requires adequate retrograde bladder filling for cystography. A routine swallow or a passively opacified bladder can miss the leak. [ACR esophagram practice parameter](https://gravitas.acr.org/PPTS/DownloadPreviewDocument?DocId=46&ReleaseId=2) · [EAU trauma guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urological-trauma/chapter/urogenital-trauma-guidelines)
3. **Airway collapse.** Separate the diaphragm sniff test from dynamic airway imaging. In a prospective pediatric study, fluoroscopy detected only 5 of 21 bronchoscopically identified cases of tracheomalacia. That result concerns the trachea. It supplies no bronchus-specific performance estimate. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21830315/)
4. **Musculoskeletal instability.** Wrist cineradiography has direct evidence for scapholunate instability. The cervical, shoulder and prosthetic-joint rows need a specified clinical setting and supporting protocol. The cervical entry is particularly misleading if read as a way to clear acute trauma. [Wrist cineradiography study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18087747/) · [ACR acute spinal trauma criteria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409895/)
5. **Observation versus diagnosis.** An observed reflux episode cannot diagnose GERD. An apple-core stricture can raise suspicion for cancer, and pharyngeal residue can suggest impaired clearance. Neither appearance identifies its cause on its own. [ACG GERD guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8754510/) · [ASHA swallow study guidance](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/resources/videofluoroscopic-swallow-study/)
6. **Selected or historical roles.** Qualify invasive venography for DVT and catheter pulmonary angiography for pulmonary embolism. Both can show filling defects, but neither is the usual initial examination. Distinguish findings actually seen during fluoroscopy from diagnoses made on subsequent CT or MRI after a fluoroscopically guided injection. [ACR lower-extremity DVT criteria](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69416/Narrative/) · [ACR pulmonary embolism criteria](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69404/Narrative/) · [ACR/RSNA arthrography guidance](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/arthrog)

## Review of every catalogue entry

The assessment column gives the interpretation or the specific change recommended for the existing text. Sources address the principal questions in each section. They do not constitute independent guideline validation of every entry.


### Esophagus (13 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Achalasia | Bird-beak tapering and retained contrast support achalasia. Manometry characterizes the motility disorder, and endoscopy assesses for a mechanical cause. |
| Diffuse esophageal spasm | Use the current term distal esophageal spasm. A corkscrew pattern can occur but is neither required nor sufficient. High-resolution manometry establishes the motility pattern. |
| Zenker diverticulum | A posterior pouch at the pharyngoesophageal junction is a characteristic contrast finding. |
| Epiphrenic diverticulum | A contrast-filled pouch arising from the distal esophagus fits this diagnosis. |
| Esophageal stricture | Persistent narrowing is visible. Its cause may require endoscopy and biopsy. |
| Esophageal carcinoma | Irregular, shouldered narrowing raises suspicion. Histology establishes malignancy. |
| Esophageal web | A thin shelf-like filling defect, often cervical, is appropriate. |
| Schatzki ring | A thin circumferential ring near the gastroesophageal junction is appropriate. |
| Hiatal hernia | Describe the portion of stomach above the hiatus. The junction’s position helps distinguish hernia types. |
| Gastroesophageal reflux | Retrograde contrast documents reflux during the examination. It cannot establish or exclude gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). |
| Esophageal perforation | Extraluminal contrast demonstrates a leak. Specify a dedicated leak esophagram, often beginning with water-soluble contrast. A negative initial study can miss a small leak. |
| Esophageal dysmotility | Abnormal propagation or clearance is a descriptive finding. Specify the pattern if assigning a particular motility disorder. |
| Esophageal fistula | Show contrast traversing a tract to a named structure. For an airway fistula, distinguish tract opacification from aspiration during swallowing. |

[ACR esophagram practice parameter](https://gravitas.acr.org/PPTS/DownloadPreviewDocument?DocId=46&ReleaseId=2) · [ACG guideline on GERD](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8754510/)

### Oropharyngeal swallowing disorders (7 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Oropharyngeal dysphagia | Impaired bolus transport, timing or clearance can be observed directly. Describe the affected phase. |
| Pulmonary aspiration | Material passing below the true vocal folds is aspiration. Review the sequence for when it occurs. |
| Laryngeal penetration | Material enters the vestibule but stays above or at the true vocal folds. This boundary distinguishes penetration from aspiration. |
| Cricopharyngeal dysfunction | Reduced opening of the pharyngoesophageal segment is visible. Several mechanisms can limit opening. |
| Pharyngeal muscle weakness | Residue alone is nonspecific. Reduced pharyngeal constriction during the swallow more directly supports weakness. |
| Delayed swallowing reflex | Prefer delayed initiation of the pharyngeal swallow. Describe timing relative to bolus position and consistency. |
| Velopharyngeal insufficiency | Contrast entering the nasopharynx can demonstrate incomplete closure during swallowing. |

[ASHA videofluoroscopic swallow study](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/resources/videofluoroscopic-swallow-study/)

### Stomach and duodenum (13 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Gastric ulcer | A contrast-filled crater is a recognized sign. Endoscopy permits biopsy of a suspicious gastric ulcer. |
| Duodenal ulcer | A bulb crater can show ulceration. Bulb deformity alone may reflect healed disease. |
| Gastric carcinoma | A filling defect, rigidity or narrowing can raise suspicion. Histological confirmation is required. |
| Gastric outlet obstruction | Focal outlet narrowing with contrast hold-up supports mechanical obstruction. Delayed emptying alone has other causes. |
| Gastric volvulus | Abnormal gastric orientation with twisting or obstruction is a recognizable upper GI finding. |
| Gastric diverticulum | Contrast delineates a pouch communicating with the stomach. |
| Linitis plastica | Poor distensibility and diffuse narrowing suggest an infiltrative process. Tissue diagnosis is needed to establish its cause. |
| Duodenal stenosis | A persistently narrowed segment demonstrates stenosis. The appearance alone may not identify its cause. |
| Duodenal diverticulum | A contrast-filled pouch communicating with the duodenum is characteristic. |
| Superior mesenteric artery syndrome | Third-part duodenal compression with proximal hold-up is suggestive. Cross-sectional imaging can show the vascular relationship. |
| Annular pancreas | Fixed second-part narrowing is an indirect clue. CT or MRI can demonstrate the encircling pancreatic tissue. |
| Intestinal malrotation | An abnormal duodenojejunal junction position supports malrotation. Positioning and the complete duodenal course matter. |
| Midgut volvulus | A corkscrew distal duodenum and proximal jejunum is a characteristic upper GI finding in the appropriate acute setting. |

[ACR upper GI examination practice parameter](https://gravitas.acr.org/PPTS/DownloadPreviewDocument?DocId=46&ReleaseId=2)

### Small intestine (7 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Crohn disease | Contrast studies can show strictures and mucosal changes. The string sign alone is nonspecific, and enterography better evaluates extraluminal disease. |
| Small bowel obstruction | Delayed transit alone is nonspecific. Distinguish a selected water-soluble contrast challenge in known obstruction from initial imaging of acute obstruction. |
| Small bowel strictures | Persistent narrowing can show the site and extent of a stricture. |
| Small bowel diverticulosis | Contrast-filled pouches communicating with small bowel are a valid finding. |
| Small bowel fistula | Contrast entering another bowel segment through an abnormal tract demonstrates an enteroenteric fistula. Other fistula destinations also occur. |
| Small bowel tumors | Filling defects and narrowing can suggest a lesion. These appearances do not establish tumor type. |
| Small bowel dysmotility | Transit is observable. Delayed progression needs interpretation in light of protocol and possible mechanical obstruction. |

### Colon and rectum (11 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Colorectal carcinoma | An apple-core stricture is a classic contrast-enema appearance. Colonoscopy and biopsy determine its cause. |
| Colonic diverticulosis | Contrast filling multiple colonic pouches is characteristic. |
| Ulcerative colitis | Loss of haustra is a classic chronic change. Avoid implying a routine contrast enema for an acute severe flare. |
| Hirschsprung disease | A transition zone and reversed rectosigmoid ratio are supportive. A normal enema does not exclude disease, and rectal biopsy confirms it. |
| Sigmoid volvulus | A tapered contrast column at the twist is recognized. Avoid implying an enema is required in every acute case. |
| Colonic strictures | A persistent narrowed segment is visible. The underlying cause may require endoscopy or cross-sectional imaging. |
| Intussusception | Specify pediatric ileocolic intussusception. Ultrasound usually makes the initial diagnosis. An air or liquid enema can demonstrate and reduce it under imaging. |
| Colonic fistula | Describe contrast traversing a tract to a named organ or cavity. |
| Colonic anastomotic leakage | Extraluminal water-soluble contrast at the anastomosis demonstrates leakage. A negative examination can miss a leak. |
| Meconium ileus | In a neonate, an unused microcolon with terminal ileal meconium pellets supports the diagnosis. Microcolon alone has other causes. |
| Small left colon syndrome | Use neonatal small left colon syndrome. An abrupt caliber change near the splenic flexure helps localize the pattern. |

### Pelvic floor and defecation disorders (7 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Rectocele | An anterior bulge during evacuation is characteristic. Size and retained contrast help assess its relevance. |
| Enterocele | Describe descent of small-bowel loops into the rectovaginal space. Oral small-bowel contrast can help identify the contents. |
| Sigmoidocele | Specify descent of redundant sigmoid colon into the pelvic cul-de-sac during evacuation. |
| Rectal prolapse | External full-thickness prolapse can be documented during evacuation. |
| Rectal intussusception | Internal infolding of the rectal wall during evacuation is directly visible. |
| Dyssynergic defecation | Impaired opening of the anorectal angle and poor evacuation support the diagnosis. Interpret with symptoms and anorectal physiological tests. |
| Descending perineum syndrome | Excessive descent during straining or evacuation is measurable against a defined pelvic landmark. |

### Urinary system (11 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Vesicoureteral reflux | Contrast reflux from bladder into ureter, sometimes reaching the collecting system, is a direct VCUG finding. |
| Posterior urethral valves | A dilated posterior urethra with an abrupt caliber change on voiding supports valves. Dilatation alone is nonspecific. |
| Urethral stricture | Retrograde urethrography can show the location and length of narrowing. |
| Urethral rupture | Contrast extravasation on a retrograde urethrogram shows urethral injury. Describe whether contrast also reaches the proximal urethra or bladder. |
| Bladder rupture | Specify adequately filled retrograde cystography. The distribution of extravasated contrast distinguishes intraperitoneal from extraperitoneal leakage. |
| Bladder diverticulum | A contrast-filled outpouching communicating with the bladder is characteristic. |
| Urethral diverticulum | A diverticulum can fill during voiding if its opening is patent. Failure to fill does not exclude it. |
| Bladder outlet obstruction | Restricted passage can localize narrowing. Pressure-flow measurements help distinguish obstruction from a weak detrusor. |
| Neurogenic bladder | Bladder contour and emptying can be abnormal for many reasons. Video-urodynamics characterizes lower urinary tract function in the neurological context. |
| Detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia | Specify involuntary sphincter activation during a detrusor contraction in a neurological disorder. Use pressure-flow data with sphincter EMG and/or synchronized imaging. |
| Urinary fistula | Show the tract or contrast entering an unexpected organ. The examination depends on the suspected site. |

[EAU urological trauma guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urological-trauma/chapter/urogenital-trauma-guidelines) · [EAU neuro-urology guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/neuro-urology)

### Female reproductive system (10 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Fallopian tube obstruction | Describe whether and where the tube fills and whether contrast spills freely. Apparent proximal occlusion can reflect transient contraction or catheter position. |
| Hydrosalpinx | A dilated contrast-filled tube supports hydrosalpinx. Record distal spill when assessing patency. |
| Intrauterine adhesions (Asherman syndrome) | Irregular, persistent cavity defects can suggest adhesions. Hysteroscopy directly assesses the cavity. |
| Septate uterus | A divided contrast-filled cavity raises suspicion. HSG cannot reliably distinguish a septate from a bicornuate uterus. Three-dimensional transvaginal ultrasound evaluates both contours. |
| Bicornuate uterus | Divergent cavities can suggest the anomaly. Classification requires the external fundal contour, which HSG does not show. |
| Unicornuate uterus | An asymmetric single cavity is suggestive. A noncommunicating rudimentary horn can be invisible on HSG. |
| Uterine fibroids | Limit the finding to submucosal or cavity-distorting fibroids. A filling defect has other possible causes. |
| Endometrial polyps | A smooth filling defect can suggest a polyp. Sonohysterography or hysteroscopy can clarify the lesion. |
| Salpingitis isthmica nodosa | A recognized tubal disorder. Small contrast-filled outpouchings in the isthmic wall are a characteristic HSG finding. |
| Peritubal adhesions | Loculated spill suggests peritubal adhesions. The adhesions themselves are inferred. |

[ASRM uterine septum guideline](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-diagnosis-and-treatment-for-uterine-septum-a-guideline-2024/) · [ASRM fertility evaluation guidance](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2026)

### Respiratory system (5 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Diaphragmatic paralysis | Paradoxical upward motion of the affected hemidiaphragm on a sniff supports unilateral paralysis. Bilateral dysfunction can be difficult to interpret. |
| Diaphragmatic weakness | Reduced excursion is observable but effort-dependent and nonspecific. |
| Diaphragmatic eventration | Elevation with altered excursion can occur. Define the morphology and acknowledge overlap with weakness or paralysis. |
| Tracheomalacia | Use dynamic airway fluoroscopy for this entry. Expiratory collapse may be visible, but fluoroscopy can miss cases. Bronchoscopy provides direct dynamic assessment. |
| Bronchomalacia | Specify dynamic expiratory assessment of a bronchus. Evidence for tracheal fluoroscopy cannot establish bronchial diagnostic accuracy. Qualify the role or supply bronchus-specific evidence. |

### Cardiovascular system (13 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Coronary artery disease | Invasive coronary angiography shows luminal stenosis. It can miss nonobstructive plaque and does not by itself measure the physiological significance of a narrowing. |
| Peripheral arterial disease | Catheter angiography depicts stenoses, occlusions and runoff in selected patients. |
| Arterial aneurysm | Contrast outlines the patent lumen. Mural thrombus can make the opacified lumen smaller than the aneurysm’s outer diameter. |
| Arteriovenous malformation | Describe feeding arteries, an intervening nidus and early draining veins in a typical high-flow malformation. |
| Arteriovenous fistula | Early venous filling through a direct communication supports a fistula. |
| Renal artery stenosis | Selective angiography can show narrowing. The image alone does not establish renovascular hypertension. |
| Carotid artery stenosis | Catheter angiography can quantify narrowing in selected cases. Duplex ultrasound and cross-sectional angiography also assess it. |
| Intracranial aneurysm | Catheter cerebral angiography can delineate the patent sac, neck and branch vessels. |
| Cerebral vasospasm | Segmental narrowing is compatible with vasospasm in the appropriate setting. Other arteriopathies can narrow the lumen. |
| Arterial dissection | Luminal irregularity or a visible flap can suggest dissection. A thrombosed false lumen may be occult. |
| Deep vein thrombosis | Use the term contrast venography. A filling defect is recognized, but duplex ultrasound is the usual initial examination for suspected lower-extremity DVT. |
| Venous stenosis | Venography shows luminal narrowing and may demonstrate collateral drainage. |
| Pulmonary embolism | Specify catheter pulmonary angiography. A filling defect is valid, but CT pulmonary angiography or ventilation-perfusion imaging usually supplies the diagnosis when imaging is indicated. |

[ACR suspected lower-extremity DVT](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69416/Narrative/) · [ACR suspected pulmonary embolism](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69404/Narrative/)

### Musculoskeletal system (6 entries)

| Chapter entry | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Carpal instability | Selected wrist cineradiography can reveal abnormal carpal motion. Specify the maneuver and suspected instability pattern. |
| Scapholunate instability | Dynamic scapholunate widening is a supported cineradiographic finding. This row is a subtype of carpal instability. |
| Cervical spine instability | Qualify or remove the broad indication. Distinguish selected specialist motion assessment from flexion-extension radiographs and from acute trauma clearance. Do not imply routine dynamic testing in suspected acute injury. |
| Shoulder instability | Abnormal translation needs a defined joint position, maneuver and clinical context. Supply a study supporting that fluoroscopic application or omit this broad entry. |
| Rotator cuff tear | On a conventional shoulder arthrogram, contrast passing from the joint into the subacromial-subdeltoid bursa supports a communicating full-thickness tear. Fluoroscopic needle placement for MR arthrography is a separate use. |
| Prosthetic joint instability | Specify a prosthesis, a stress maneuver and the abnormal relationship shown, or omit the broad entry. Component motion relative to bone can suggest loosening. Instability concerns the relationship between the articulating components. |
