\chapter{Mammography}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Mammography uses low-dose X-rays to create detailed images of the internal structure of the breast. It is designed to detect microcalcifications or masses that may indicate breast cancer.

\section*{Alternative Names}
Mammogram, Breast X-ray

\section*{Principle}
Mammography uses low-dose X-rays to create detailed images of the internal structure of the breast. It is designed to detect microcalcifications or masses that may indicate breast cancer.

\section*{History}
Albert Salomon performed the first breast X-rays in 1913. Mammography became a standardized screening method in the 1960s with the introduction of dedicated low-dose X-ray machines.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
Ordered as a routine screening for breast cancer in women over 40 (or earlier for high-risk patients) or as a diagnostic tool for breast lumps, pain, or nipple discharge.

\section*{Is This a Primary or Secondary Test}
Primary screening tool for early detection of breast cancer.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
You stand in front of an X-ray machine. Your breast is placed on a flat plate and compressed with a plastic paddle to spread out the tissue. Images are taken from multiple angles.

\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
Do not apply deodorant, powder, lotion, or perfume under your arms or on your breasts on the day of the test, as they can show up as calcium spots.

\section*{Contraindications and Precautions}
Pregnancy (unless diagnostic necessity overrides risks). Breast implants require special displacement views.

\section*{Risks}
Minimal exposure to ionizing radiation. Temporary discomfort or pain from breast compression during the procedure.

\section*{What Happens After the Test}
You can immediately resume normal activities. Results are interpreted using the BI-RADS classification system.

\section*{Understanding Your Results}
BI-RADS 1 is negative, BI-RADS 2 is benign. BI-RADS 0 requires additional imaging. BI-RADS 4 or 5 is suspicious and warrants biopsy.

\section*{Normal Results}
BI-RADS 1 (Negative) or BI-RADS 2 (Benign findings like simple cysts or stable fibroadenomas).

\section*{Follow-up Tests}
Breast ultrasound, breast MRI, or breast biopsy.
