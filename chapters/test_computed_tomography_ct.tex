\chapter{Computed Tomography (CT)}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
A computed tomography (CT) scan combines a series of X-ray images taken from different angles around your body and uses computer processing to create cross-sectional images (slices) of bones, blood vessels, and soft tissues.

\section*{Alternative Names}
CT scan, CAT scan

\section*{Principle}
A computed tomography (CT) scan combines a series of X-ray images taken from different angles around your body and uses computer processing to create cross-sectional images (slices) of bones, blood vessels, and soft tissues.

\section*{History}
Invented by Godfrey Hounsfield and Allan Cormack in 1972, for which they received the Nobel Prize in 1979. The technology revolutionized diagnostic imaging.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
Ordered to diagnose bone fractures, detect tumors, locate blood clots or internal bleeding, guide biopsies, and monitor cancer treatments.

\section*{Is This a Primary or Secondary Test}
Primary diagnostic tool for acute trauma, suspected stroke, and pulmonary embolism.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
You lie on a table that slides into a doughnut-shaped scanner. You must remain still and may be asked to hold your breath briefly. Contrast dye may be given IV or orally.

\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
Preparation varies by protocol. Fasting is required only when specifically instructed, such as for selected sedated or gastrointestinal studies. Tell the imaging team about prior contrast reactions, kidney disease, and pregnancy. Shellfish allergy does not by itself predict a reaction to iodinated contrast.

\section*{Contraindications and Precautions}
Pregnancy is not an automatic contraindication; the examination should be justified and optimized. Review prior severe contrast reactions and kidney function when IV contrast is planned.

\section*{Risks}
Ionizing radiation is used. Contrast reactions are uncommon but can be severe; kidney injury risk depends on baseline kidney function and clinical context.

\section*{What Happens After the Test}
Drink plenty of fluids if you received contrast dye to help flush it from your kidneys. Resume normal diet and medications.

\section*{Understanding Your Results}
Normal scans show healthy structures with no masses, hemorrhage, or fractures. Contrast enhancement patterns help identify lesions.

\section*{Normal Results}
Normal anatomical structures; no abnormal mass, hemorrhage, fluid collection, or vascular occlusion.

\section*{Follow-up Tests}
MRI, tissue biopsy, or follow-up CT scan.
